Sidebar

An app's navigation column beside its page — folded to a strip of icons, or sliding in as a sheet on a phone.

Examples

The column

Sidebar is a column 256 points wide on the sidebar's fill, its edge against the page, holding a SidebarHeader, the groups in a SidebarContent that scrolls when they do not fit, and a SidebarFooter.

Three axes say how it meets the window:

  • side — left (default) or right: which edge it stands on.
  • variant — sidebar (default) its own edge against the page; floating a card standing in the window's air, 8 points of room around it; inset a card on the sidebar's ground, with the page's own card beside it on that same ground, 8 points from it and from the window's edges.
  • collapsible — offcanvas (default) leaves the window when it closes; icon folds to a strip of icons (48 points); none never closes.

Closing takes the room away at once: the platform transitions colors, opacity and transforms, not layout, so there is no 200 ms width animation to land (and no motion-reduce to switch off) — the column is either as wide as its state says, or gone.

The page beside it

SidebarInset is the page, filling what is left of the window: the page's own fill, or in the inset variant a card with its corners 8 points from the column's card and from the window's edges. Room for the header of the page is the app's: a row with the trigger, a Separator (orientation="vertical", 16 high), where you are, and what it holds.

The open state

SidebarProvider owns it: open (controlled) or defaultOpen (default true), reported by onOpenChange({ open, reason }) — triggerPress, railPress or shortcut. Nothing is written down: storage is the app's, which usually keeps the state where it keeps the rest of its settings and passes it back as open.

useSidebar() is what the parts read, and what an app reads inside the provider: state ("expanded" or "collapsed"), open, openMobile, isMobile, setOpen, setOpenMobile and toggleSidebar. Every value is live: read it in a JSX binding and it follows the provider.

⌘B on Apple platforms, Ctrl+B elsewhere, folds the column and opens it — keymap.bind("mod+b"), made where the provider draws and gone with it. The rail at the column's inner edge takes a press too: it is 8 points wide (16 wide to a press, past its bounds with hitSlop) and shows a hairline in the sidebar's edge color while the pointer is over it. That hairline follows the pointer through the class language (group-hover:), not a signal, so passing the cursor over the rail commits no frame; the pointer has to be on the 8-point strip itself, not only in its hitSlop margin. SidebarTrigger is the button that usually sits in the page's header.

On a phone

A touch screen, or a window narrower than 600 points, shows the column as a sheet instead — this library's Sheet, 288 points wide from the side the column stands on, with the same trigger opening it. The column's own state is left alone, so a window that widens again finds it as it was.

A row

SidebarMenuButton is a row of SidebarMenu, inside a SidebarGroupContent of a SidebarGroup that a SidebarGroupLabel names: the theme's row high (controlSize.row), its label the body type role, the pointer's fill under it, the sidebarAccent fill when isActive (the page the app is on), the keyboard's ring, and 50% while disabled. It takes a press (onPress), or it is a row of its own; no href, as a navigation menu's link has none.

size is the row's step — default the theme's row, sm the height step under it, lg a team's switcher or a user's row, 48 points where the row is a pointer's (a touch screen's row is already a finger's height) — and variant is its look: default, bare until the pointer is over it, or outline, a card of its own on the column.

A string child is the row's name. Anything else is drawn as given, which is how an icon leads a name: the look has the words for it.

Folded to icons, the row is a square the height of a row with its icon, and tooltip names it on a desktop: a string is the name, or the tooltip's own props (placement, delay, …). It also names the row for screen readers when nothing else does — and it replaces nsh/base's element tooltip, which is what this prop is.

A row keeps its name only while the column is open: nothing else moves, so a look is written once where the row draws. A row the app draws hands its look the column's fold — open and collapsible, the app's own signals — and its name goes; a look given no fold options draws the open row, and an action or a badge inside a folded item leaves the tree.

An action, a badge

SidebarMenuAction is a 20-point square at the row's trailing edge, in SidebarMenuItem, and SidebarMenuBadge a pill there — a count, a short word, its numbers tabular. The item keeps the room they need, so the row's name stops short of them; both on one row, the action steps in by the badge's width rather than drawing over it.

An action is always there: hover alone is something a touch screen has no way to do.

A sub menu

SidebarMenuSub holds the rows under a row that opens them — SidebarMenuSubItem and its SidebarMenuSubButton, the height step under the row (controlHeight.md). It is 14 points in from the row above it, with a hairline down its start in the sidebar's edge color, and it hides while the column is folded.

The row above it opens it. nsh/base's Collapsible does the reveal — not this library's Collapsible, which draws a disclosure row of its own (a settings list wants that; a sidebar row wants the row it already has):

A group

SidebarGroupLabel names a group in 32 points of quiet type; folded to icons it takes no room and draws nothing, and stays in the tree for a screen reader. SidebarGroupAction stands at its trailing edge — a search, a settings — 20 points square. SidebarGroupContent takes the rows, as wide as the group.

A row that is still coming

SidebarMenuSkeleton is the skeleton's blocks where an icon and a name will be: an icon's box when showIcon, then a line of text as long as width says (70% when it says nothing). A length made up per mount is a picture that changes between runs, so this one is told.

API

The provider owns the state and lays the column and the page out; a column and its parts draw on nsh's View (SidebarSeparator wraps this library's Separator, and the phone's sheet is this library's Sheet). Every nsh view prop passes through; a row is an nsh/base button's props too.
